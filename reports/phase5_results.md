(T3 holdout XGB ROC-AUC 0.671; T4 holdout XGB 0.899, hist-GBM 0.897 — tied).

**Honest headline:** the peer-relative target (Y2) is substantially more predictable
than the standardised-return target (Y1). **XGBoost does not dominate**: histogram
gradient boosting (Optuna-tuned, §3.7 of Ch.3) is best on the USD Y2 holdout and tied on
nominal Y2, and logistic regression is competitive.
Persistence is a weak baseline for Y1 (holdout 0.233) but a moderate one for Y2 (0.711).

## 5. Inferential analysis (Phase 6) — `reports/phase6_lead_lag.csv`

Lower rainfall anomalies are weakly associated with **higher** next-month returns.
Strongest at a **one-month** lead: r = −0.112 (usdprice), −0.124 (nominal), n = 12,520,
p < 0.001. The dissertation's fabricated "r = −0.22 at two-month lead" is **not reproduced**;
the real association is roughly half as strong and peaks at one month.

## 7. Permutation importance (holdout) — `artifacts/perm_importance_*.csv`

- **Y1 (XGBoost):** `spi` (+0.088), `ret_lag1` (+0.038), `rain_anom_lag3` (+0.033),
  `rain_anom_z` (+0.014), interaction (+0.010). Rainfall features contribute modestly;
  several features have negative means (redundancy/instability) — flagged as such.
- **Y2 (hist-GBM):** `spi` dominates (+0.244, expected: the target is the next SPI),
  then `category` (+0.054), `peer_count` (+0.028); rainfall effects are small (≤ 0.008).

## 8. What this means (stated plainly)

- It is **not** established that food price stress can be predicted reliably. Y1 is
  weak (holdout ROC-AUC ≈ 0.59–0.67). Y2 is moderate-to-good but partly because the
  current peer-relative price is persistent.
- Rainfall carries only **weak** incremental signal.
- No model is suitable for operational early warning without prospective testing.
